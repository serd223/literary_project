Even before I had put on that robe, I knew that—somewhere deep inside— something wasn’t
right. When they asked, “What are you up to?” I would take refuge behind the fortress of “I’m
a student,” but there were cracks in those walls. The view outside was unsettling. Still, back
then I had a perfect escape: “I don’t think about it now,” I would tell myself. “Let the
midterms pass, let me get that diploma—then I’ll deal with the rest.” School was like a mist
that both shielded me from the outside world and kept me from facing it. Remaining within
that fog felt safer than joining the line of the unemployed waiting beyond it.
