It’s midnight. In the silence of the room, I hear not just the ticking of the clock, but as if something outside is slowly crumbling, falling to pieces. They call it social decay; but what I feel is more of a void. Everything is slowly being hollowed out from within. Friendships have turned into alliances of self-interest, and greetings into mere attempts to prove we’re still here. It’s as if, with each passing day, the ground we stand on grows a little muddier. Whatever we used to call wrong has now become a rule for survival. It’s as if no one owes anyone anything anymore, and no one has the luxury of loving another simply because they’re human. Everyone is trying to clear their own path by pushing someone else aside. 
The only thing this system promises us is an endless waiting room. In that room, no one is anyone else’s companion. Everyone is preoccupied with stepping on others’ shoulders to climb a little higher, to catch a little more breath. On one hand, we’re forced to keep up with everything, to look good everywhere; on the other, we know we’re not actually getting anywhere. The worst part is this: being fully aware of everything—every absurdity—yet being unable to change a single thing. A person turns into a spectator watching their own life from the outside.  
The greatest thing this system demands of us is that we accept this sense of alienation and loneliness as some kind of failure. They want us to doubt ourselves. Yet this unease may well be our strongest instinct within a broken system. The real problem is finding peace within a decaying structure. Yet if we still feel the pain, that is the only proof that we are still alive, that we have not become part of this rubble. 
That is why knowing that someone else is thinking the exact same things right now is not a blessing, but a necessity. Trying to reach out to someone in this chaos is not a romantic fantasy; it is the only way out of this decay. Because I know that, while everyone lives their own lie, bearing witness to another’s genuine pain is the only honest act we can perform against the system. This isn’t about salvation; it’s simply the way to avoid getting lost in this rubble. Perhaps the real issue isn’t finding an escape route, but realizing that this chaos we’re in isn’t unique to us. That’s all.
