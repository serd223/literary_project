Even before I had put on that robe, I knew that—somewhere deep inside— something wasn’t right. When they asked, “What are you up to?” I would take refuge behind the fortress of “I’m a student,” but there were cracks in those walls. The view outside was unsettling. Still, back then I had a perfect escape: “I don’t think about it now,” I would tell myself. “Let the midterms pass, let me get that diploma—then I’ll deal with the rest.” School was like a mist that both shielded me from the outside world and kept me from facing it. Remaining within that fog felt safer than joining the line of the unemployed waiting beyond it.
And then, in a single day, the mist dissolved. The caps were thrown into the air, congratulations were exchanged, and that word I had trusted so much—“then”—stepped forward and stood right in front of me. There was no midterm week left to hide behind, no “let me just finish this assignment” to cling to. When I took off the robe and set it aside, I realized that the title of “student” had slipped away from me as well—and I didn’t even know what remained in its place.
Now, I am in that infamous “in-between.” Every morning I open my computer to forms, exam dates, well-meaning wishes of “I wish it all turn out well”… Outside, the world keeps spinning, everyone seems to be rushing somewhere. And I stand at the very center of uncertainty. As I try to fit myself into the system out there, I feel myself slowly losing the person within. The “me” outside has to succeed, has to become something respectable. But at night, when my head touches the pillow, that same question lingers:
Is this person I’ve become truly me—or just a stranger shaped by the noise of the outside world?
Looking back, I now understand that the fog didn’t only hide me—it also kept me from seeing the world. Inside it, there were only my own struggles and the muffled voices of others. When the curtain finally lifted, I began to see the world in its bare reality. It wasn’t as terrifying as the exaggerated darkness of social media, but neither was it the rosy tale I had been told.
I feel myself coming to peace. In my ears, a familiar melody echoes: “Dön bak dünyaya” (Turn and look at the world) (Pinhani). This new landscape no longer frightens me. On the contrary, stripped of all the labels that once defined me, I begin to ask: Is my life only just beginning? Perhaps now it is time to set out in search for “me” that has ben silently waiting, somewhere deep inside.
